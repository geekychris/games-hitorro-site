Hitorro Games

Relive your childhood adventures.

Hitorro Games makes adventures like the ones we grew up with: explore strange places, meet odd people, work out what is going on, and see where the story takes you. Every place, person and creature has its own painting, and each game has music written for it. Puzzles have more than one answer, and the endings are earned.

Four games are in testing now, and two arcade adventures are on the way. They're coming to Mac and Windows, and most of them to AmigaOS 4, because some of us never left.

Adventures

Science-fiction mystery

The Last Broadcast

North Point. Come alone. It is happening again.

Nigel P. Wimple takes a cheap off-season break on Blackwater Island. On the first evening the hotel television speaks to him, in his own voice. An atmospheric, wry mystery of old radio, older secrets and a station on the cliff that should have stayed off the air.

Satirical science fiction

Army of Old Men

Medicare or Military Care. Your choice.

Five old friends sign up for the army because it's the only way left to get their hips seen to. Then the aliens arrive. Sixteen chapters of squad tactics, bad knees, worse jokes and a war that might yet end at a peace table, if you can keep everyone together long enough to get there.

Arcade adventure · twenty-one chapters

Iso Blaster: The Null Crown

One courier, one small ship, and a fortress that ate a moon.

A machine fortress has woken on the moon of the jungle world Verdance, and it is draining the planet grey. Ash Vey flies parcels for a living. Her plan is to slip past the guns, get inside, and switch off the Null Crown at its heart by hand. It does not go to plan.

The story moves between the styles of play we grew up on: a scrolling shooter seen from above at an angle, from straight overhead and from the cockpit, with the view changing in mid-flight; a crash-landing threaded down through the jungle canopy; a jungle run of vines, tar pits and snapping jaws; a temple held against waves of drones from the twin guns on its steps; a dugout chased down a black river; one-on-one duels, bare-handed and then with swords; repairs to the ship in a scavenger's hangar; a fortress explored room by room on foot; and the Crown itself, a lattice of pipes and power to be cut apart before the warden wakes. The pictures are painted, the music is written note by note and played the old way, as a four-channel tracker module, and every chapter comes with the level designer it was built in.

Arcade adventure · nine chapters

Stormlight: The Keeper of Gull Rock

One night, one dark lamp, and eleven boats still out on the Shoal.

On the night of the great storm, lightning puts out the Gull Rock light while the fishing fleet is still at sea, and a red rocket goes up off Ruddock Stack where the trawler Kittiwake has struck. Mara Quill, keeper of the light, has to get the crew off the ledge, get the lifeboat home, and hold the harbour against Jory Slake's Gaff Men, who have come out on kites and launches to strip the wrecks. Then she has to climb the tower and light the lamp.

Stormlight is the second game built on the Iso Blaster engine, with four styles of play of its own: a gyrocopter flown down the cliffs past gusts, slate falls and kite-gliders; the lifeboat run back through the reef; the old brass gun on the harbour wall; and the lamp room, where paraffin, steam and current have to be piped to the burners before the storm glass bottoms out. The music is sea shanties and storm tunes, played as four-channel tracker modules.

Fantasy adventures

Solo adventures with 5e-compatible rules: make a character, roll the dice, and play a tabletop-style story on your own. Everything you need is in the game; no rulebook required.

One character, levels 1 to 3 · one to two hours

The Bell Under Hollin's Ford

A drowned bell, a forgetful village, and a roll call nobody answered.

On still nights a bell tolls from under the river, where the old chapel went down ninety-nine years ago, and each time it does, someone in the village forgets a name. Play a fighter, rogue, cleric or wizard, hire a companion, and go under the river. Almost every problem has more than one answer, and there are five endings.

One-shot for a level-3 adventurer · about an hour

Salt and Glass

Tides, lenses, and a client who should not be trusted.

A glass-wright's tower stands at the end of a causeway the sea takes back twice a day. A Guild factor will pay a hundred and fifty gold for a lens from "a madwoman" inside, before tonight's spring tide closes the way. No questions. Every fight has another way through, and the tide will not wait.
