Hitorro Games

Stories you play by reading, typing and thinking.

Hitorro Games makes illustrated text adventures: you read a scene, type what you want to do, and the story answers. Every place, person and creature has its own painting, and each game has music written for it. Puzzles have more than one answer, and the endings are earned.

Four games are in testing now. They're coming to Mac and Windows, and three of them to AmigaOS 4, because some of us never left.

Adventures

Science-fiction mystery

The Last Broadcast

North Point. Come alone. It is happening again.

Nigel P. Wimple takes a cheap off-season break on Blackwater Island. On the first evening the hotel television speaks to him, in his own voice. An atmospheric, wry mystery of old radio, older secrets and a station on the cliff that should have stayed off the air.

Satirical science fiction

Army of Old Men

Medicare or Military Care. Your choice.

Five old friends sign up for the army because it's the only way left to get their hips seen to. Then the aliens arrive. Sixteen chapters of squad tactics, bad knees, worse jokes and a war that might yet end at a peace table, if you can keep everyone together long enough to get there.

In testing Mac · Windows · iPad

Fantasy adventures

Solo adventures with 5e-compatible rules: make a character, roll the dice, and play a tabletop-style story on your own. Everything you need is in the game; no rulebook required.

One character, levels 1 to 3 · one to two hours

The Bell Under Hollin's Ford

A drowned bell, a forgetful village, and a roll call nobody answered.

On still nights a bell tolls from under the river, where the old chapel went down ninety-nine years ago, and each time it does, someone in the village forgets a name. Play a fighter, rogue, cleric or wizard, hire a companion, and go under the river. Almost every problem has more than one answer, and there are five endings.

In testing Mac · Windows · AmigaOS 4

One-shot for a level-3 adventurer · about an hour

Salt and Glass

Tides, lenses, and a client who should not be trusted.

A glass-wright's tower stands at the end of a causeway the sea takes back twice a day. A Guild factor will pay a hundred and fifty gold for a lens from "a madwoman" inside, before tonight's spring tide closes the way. No questions. Every fight has another way through, and the tide will not wait.
